Draft licensing rules.How to audit the file.

Draft regulations for the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act are expected in late 2026. To secure a licence and maintain enhanced Housing Benefit, providers must pass a fit and proper person test and prove strict compliance with the National Supported Housing Standards through rigorous support file auditing.

Quick brief

  • The deadline. Consultation on the draft regulations for the Supported Housing Act is expected in late 2026.
  • The condition. Meeting the National Supported Housing Standards will be a mandatory licence condition for most providers.
  • The assessment rule. A key licence condition will require resident needs assessments to be completed within four weeks of move-in.
  • The audit action. Providers must run internal mock audits now to ensure support notes clearly link back to assessed needs and goals.

The National Standards become mandatory

The runway for compliance in the Supported Exempt Accommodation (SEA) sector is rapidly disappearing. With the government expecting to consult on the draft regulations for the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act in late 2026, the framework for local authority inspections is taking shape.1,2

The most critical update for operations managers and auditors is that meeting the National Supported Housing Standards will be a strict licence condition. Furthermore, the government intends to link the payment of enhanced Housing Benefit directly to holding this licence. If your support files cannot prove you meet the standards, your funding will be restricted to Local Housing Allowance levels.1

Auditing to survive the fit and proper test

To secure a licence, directors, partners, and nominated individuals must pass a rigorous fit and proper person test, which will be enhanced when dealing with vulnerable residents. Passing this test requires impeccable management records.1

Auditing your resident files is no longer a best-practice exercise; it is a survival mechanism. If your operation still manages resident support files on loose paper and disparate spreadsheets, the incoming licensing regulations will expose those systemic weaknesses immediately.

To benchmark your setup ahead of 2027, your internal audits must verify the following:

  • The four-week rule. You must prove that initial needs assessments happen within four weeks of a resident moving in. This is a confirmed licence condition.1
  • Visible support. Your session notes must show exactly what support was given towards which specific goal. Simply noting that a conversation occurred is not enough.
  • Linked goals. Every support goal documented must logically follow from an assessed need and be explicitly agreed upon with the resident.
  • Scheduled reviews. Your 3, 6 and 9-month reviews must be completed on schedule, signed and dated by both the resident and staff.

Providers who survive this transition will be those who automate compliance—using digital tools to ensure their case logging stays audit-ready every single day. See what a good resident support file contains.

Frequently asked questions

What are the National Supported Housing Standards?

The National Supported Housing Standards rest on five core principles: person-centred, respectful, safe and responsive, effective, and well-led, setting the benchmark for what good support looks like.1

Will commissioned services need to meet the National Standards?

While most commissioned services will still need to hold a licence, they will not be required to comply with the National Supported Housing Standards licensing condition, as there is already oversight of the support service.1,2
